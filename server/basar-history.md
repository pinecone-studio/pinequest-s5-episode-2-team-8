REFERENCE SHEET: the life of Chinggis Khaan (Чингис хаан), for Basar's answers.
Use it to answer accurately and in line with the game's story chapters. Use the Mongolian names below when answering in Mongolian.
Where this sheet says historians disagree, say so simply. Do not add dates, numbers or quotes that are not here or not well established.
Keep it child-friendly: battles and hardships can be named, never described in a graphic way.

SOURCES
- The Secret History of the Mongols (Монголын нууц товчоо) is the oldest Mongolian literary work. It tells the ancestry and life of Chinggis Khaan and the start of Ögedei's reign. Usually it is said to have 12 chapters and 282 sections. It was written in the 13th century, soon after his death; the exact year is debated (1228 and 1240 are both proposed). The original Mongol-script text is lost; it survives in a Chinese-character transcription of the Mongolian.
- Other sources: Rashid al-Din's Compendium of Chronicles (Persian), Chinese records, and travellers such as Giovanni da Pian del Carpine and William of Rubruck.

ANCESTRY
- The Secret History opens with the Blue-grey Wolf (Бөртэ чоно) and the Fallow Doe (Гуа марал), the legendary first ancestors, who came to the Onon river near Burkhan Khaldun (Бурхан Халдун).
- Alan Gua (Алан гуа) is a legendary ancestress. Her lesson of the arrows: one arrow breaks easily, five arrows tied together do not. Brothers who stay together are strong. (This is Alan Gua's story, often wrongly told about Hoelun.)

CHILDHOOD (the game's first chapters)
- Name at birth: Temüjin (Тэмүжин). Father: Yesügei baatar (Есүхэй баатар), a chief of the Borjigin (Боржигин) clan. Mother: Hoelun (Өэлүн үжин).
- Born at Delüün Boldog (Дэлүүн болдог) by the Onon river. The year is debated: Mongolia officially uses 1162; other proposals are 1155 and 1167. The Secret History says he was born holding a clot of blood the size of a knucklebone in his right hand.
- He was named after a Tatar leader, Temüjin Üge, whom Yesügei had just captured.
- At about nine, Yesügei took him to the Onggirat (Хонгирад) people to find him a bride. Dei Sechen (Дэй сэцэн) chose his daughter Börte (Бөртэ) for him, and Temüjin stayed with her family. On the way home Yesügei ate with Tatars, who poisoned him; he died at home.
- The Tayichiud (Тайчууд) left Hoelun and her children behind on the steppe. Hoelun kept the family alive by gathering wild fruit, roots and plants, and the boys fished and hunted marmots. Hoelun is remembered as a brave, wise mother.
- The Tayichiud later captured Temüjin and put a wooden collar (cangue, мод хүзүүвч) on him. He escaped at night by hiding in the Onon river. Sorkhan Shira (Сорхон шар), a Süldüs man, saw him and kept the secret, and his family hid Temüjin in a cart of wool and helped him get home.
- When thieves took the family's eight horses, Temüjin followed them. On the way he met Boorchu (Боорчу), a boy who left his own herd to help him. Boorchu became his first and most loyal companion, later a great general.
- Jamukha (Жамуха) was his childhood friend and sworn brother (анд). As boys by the Onon they swapped knucklebones (шагай) and arrows.

BECOMING A LEADER
- Temüjin married Börte. Her family's wedding gift was a black sable coat; he gave it to Toghrul (Тоорил), khan of the Kereit (Хэрэйд), his father's sworn brother, and won his support. Toghrul later received the Chinese title Wang Khan (Ван хан).
- The Merkit (Мэргид) raided the camp and carried Börte away; Temüjin hid on Burkhan Khaldun and afterwards honoured the mountain. With Toghrul and Jamukha he defeated the Merkit and brought Börte back.
- Temüjin and Jamukha later went separate ways and became rivals; in 1201 Jamukha's allies named him Gür khan. Temüjin also later broke with Toghrul, and defeated the Kereit (1203) and the Naiman (Найман) (1204). Jamukha was finally captured and died (1205). The Secret History says Temüjin offered him friendship again and Jamukha asked to die without bloodshed; tell this gently, if at all.
- He rewarded people for loyalty and skill rather than noble birth, and took in people from the clans he defeated. Followers like Boorchu, Mukhali (Мухулай), Jelme (Зэлмэ), Subutai (Сүбээдэй) and Jebe (Зэв) became his famous generals.

1206 AND THE GREAT MONGOL STATE
- In 1206 a Great Khuriltai (Их хуралдай) at the source of the Onon river proclaimed Temüjin "Chinggis Khaan" and founded the Great Mongol State (Их Монгол Улс). A white standard with nine tails (есөн хөлт цагаан туг) was raised. The meaning of the name "Chinggis" is debated (ideas include "strong, firm" and "ocean-like, universal").
- The army and people were organised in tens, hundreds, thousands and ten-thousands (аравт, зуут, мянгат, түмэн).
- The Great Law (Их засаг) set rules for the realm; its full text has not survived.
- Writing: in 1204 Tata-tonga (Тататунга), a Uyghur scribe of the defeated Naiman, began teaching the Uyghur script to the Mongols. This became the classical Mongol script (монгол бичиг), written from top to bottom.
- A relay post system of stations with fresh horses (өртөө) carried messages quickly; it was expanded under Ögedei.
- Merchants and travellers were protected, and many religions were tolerated.

CAMPAIGNS (name them, do not describe violence)
- Western Xia (Tangut, Тангуд) from 1209; the Jin dynasty in northern China from 1211 (Zhongdu, today's Beijing, fell in 1215); the Khwarazmian Empire in Central Asia 1219 to 1221, after its governor killed a Mongol trade caravan and envoys.
- The empire under Chinggis Khaan and his sons became the largest land empire in history by the 13th century (its greatest size came later, under his grandsons).

FAMILY
- Börte was his chief wife. Their four sons: Jochi (Зүчи), Chagatai (Цагаадай), Ögedei (Өгөдэй) and Tolui (Тулуй). He chose Ögedei as heir; Ögedei became Great Khan in 1229. Grandsons include Batu, Möngke and Khubilai (Хубилай), who founded the Yuan dynasty.

DEATH AND MEMORY
- He died in 1227 during the last campaign against the Western Xia. The cause is uncertain (a fall from his horse and illness are both reported).
- His burial place is unknown and was kept secret; many think it is in the Khentii mountains near Burkhan Khaldun, but no one has found it.
- Burkhan Khaldun and its sacred landscape are a UNESCO World Heritage Site (2015).
- In Mongolia he is honoured as the founder of the nation: the main airport is named Chinggis Khaan International Airport, there is a large equestrian statue at Tsonjin Boldog (Цонжин болдог), and his portrait is on the money. His birthday is celebrated on the first day of the first winter month of the lunar calendar (Их Эзэн Чингис хааны өдөр, a national holiday since 2012).
- Historians see him both as a brilliant founder and organiser and as a conqueror whose wars caused great suffering. If asked, say both simply and kindly.
